# Phase 1 Step 2 — Deep Research Checkpoint — 2026-08-24

## Product implication

Nivasa must treat India's interior market as a fragmented evidence graph, not a conventional catalogue. Current research identifies fragmented supply, inconsistent specifications/pricing, independent designers, small workshops, local vendors, organized platforms, manufacturers and specialist channels as structural characteristics of the market.

## Step 2 implementation consequence

Reference sources are backend evidence. They must be transformed into auditable observations and then into canonical product, price and design intelligence.

Required evidence chain:

source → observation → evidence → normalized entity → product/SKU/variant → price observation → confidence/freshness → comparison/alternative → user explanation

## Coverage rule

Coverage must include both major and small decisions: furniture, wardrobes, kitchens, hardware, fittings, sanitaryware, bath, plumbing, surfaces, paints, lighting, electrical, smart home, appliances, furnishings, rugs, curtains, décor, art, paintings, mandir/pooja, Indian crafts, accessories, local fabrication, labour, logistics and installation.

## Current external research signals

- Mordor Intelligence (August 2026) describes the Indian interior design supply side as highly fragmented and price-competitive, with transactions still flowing through unorganized carpenters and small workshops; it also identifies AI/AR/VR and real-time pricing as technology directions.
- NSE-filed industry material identifies lack of discovery, weak standardization/transparency/pricing, fragmented supply chains and difficulty comparing service providers as home-interior market problems.
- Euromonitor's May 2026 India home-products research describes home-products specialists as highly fragmented with limited consolidation.
- NIQ's August 2026 smart-home research highlights interoperability, setup complexity and trust as barriers to connected living adoption in India.

These sources validate the Nivasa thesis but do not replace primary product evidence. Research reports are market context only; product facts must come from product/manufacturer/retailer/vendor evidence and must retain provenance.

## Hard guardrails

1. Never invent product facts.
2. Never invent warranty claims.
3. Never present an estimate as a verified price.
4. Preserve price type, unit, geography, timestamp and evidence.
5. Preserve source freshness and confidence.
6. Do not equate a design reference with a purchasable product.
7. Do not equate two products merely because their names are similar.
8. Preserve alternatives and explain material/specification differences.
9. Keep source governance separate from user-facing presentation.
10. Step 2 is complete only when the executable pipeline, persistence, tests and CI gates support these rules.
